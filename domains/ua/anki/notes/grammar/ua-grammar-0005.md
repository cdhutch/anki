  Topic: Prefixed Motion Verbs — Spelling at the Prefix/Root Boundary
  Text: 'At the boundary before the vehicle root -їхати/-їжджати (вʼїхати, підʼїхати, відʼїхати;
    переїхати, доїхати, заїхати): a prefix that ends in {{c1::a hard consonant}} takes {{c2::an
    apostrophe}}. A prefix that ends in {{c3::a vowel}} needs {{c4::nothing extra}}, since
    ї already carries the glide.'
  Extra: 'General rule: an apostrophe marks a hard-consonant prefix ending directly before
    a root starting with я/ю/є/ї -- the letter already encodes the glide, so nothing else
    is added. This matches Ukrainian orthography beyond PVOM too, e.g. зʼїзд (з- + їзд, and
    з isn''t even labial) -- so it''s not a labials-only rule. Vowel-ending prefixes (ви-,
    при-, до-, про-, пере-, за-, по-) never take an apostrophe here, since there''s no hard-consonant/glide
    collision to mark. The walking root йти/ходити is orthogonal to this rule entirely --
    ходити (imperfective) starts with the plain consonant х, and йти (perfective) simply starts
    with й as its own first letter; neither ever takes an apostrophe, and the й in forms like
    перейти́ or увійти́ is not the same phenomenon as the ʼ in підʼїхати. Parallel in Russian:
    ъ hard sign -- въехать, подъехать, отъехать.'
