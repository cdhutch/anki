# Slovak Letter → IPA Card Data — Round 3

Round 3 card type: front = a Slovak letter, digraph, or diphthong spelling,
back = the IPA symbol(s) it represents plus a basic example word. As with
Czech, most letters reuse sounds you already know from Round 1 or Round 2;
only the genuinely new items are called out below.

entire consonant systems, including c/ť, ɟ/ď, and ɲ/ň.

## Context-dependent palatalization rule

The vowel letters e, i, í, and the diphthong spellings ie/ia trigger a
palatal pronunciation of a preceding d, n, t, or l — the same
"soft-vowel" mechanism as Czech — with exceptions for many loanwords and a
handful of fixed grammatical endings (e.g. masculine nominative plurals).

## A phonetic note on š / ž / č / dž

Slovak's š/ž/č/dž are pronounced slightly further back (closer to
retroflex, [ʂ ʐ tʂ dʐ]) than Czech's or Ukrainian's versions of the same
letters. This table uses the same simplified symbols (ʃ ʒ tʃ dʒ) as Round 1
and Round 2 for consistency and comparability — the real, narrower
phonetic difference is worth knowing about but isn't worth a separate row.

## Sound-alternation notes (not separate cards)

Slovak has extensive final devoicing and voicing assimilation (e.g. b→p,
d→t at word end or before a voiceless consonant) and a "rhythmic law"
preventing two long syllables in a row within a word. These are
pronunciation *rules*, not additional phonemes, so they don't get their own
front/back cards — but they explain why the same letter can sound voiced in
one word and voiceless in another.
